# V1-V4 Diagnostic Grammar

The V1-V4 diagnostic grammar defines the public SORT analysis-layer movement from observation to decision relevance.

## Overview

| Dimension | Public Meaning | Function |
|---|---|---|
| $V_1$ | Observed structural phenomenon | what becomes visible |
| $V_2$ | Structural cause or coupling surface | why the phenomenon is structurally induced |
| $V_3$ | Effect, projection, or risk space | how the structural condition becomes assessable |
| $V_4$ | Decision or evidence surface | what kind of decision or evidence state becomes possible |

## Diagnostic Movement

$$
V_1
\rightarrow
V_2
\rightarrow
V_3
\rightarrow
V_4.
$$

This movement is not a causal mechanism and not a runtime algorithm. It is a public diagnostic grammar.

## V1 — Observed Structural Phenomenon

$V_1$ identifies the phenomenon visible at the system level.

Examples include degraded effective throughput, retry amplification, structural drift, boundary occupation, cost escalation, or reduced reproducibility despite locally correct components.

## V2 — Structural Cause or Coupling Surface

$V_2$ identifies the structural coupling surface that makes the phenomenon intelligible.

Examples include physical coupling, runtime-control coupling, semantic/agentic coupling, model-boundary coupling, evidence-surface coupling, or cross-layer coupling.

## V3 — Effect, Projection, or Risk Space

$V_3$ expresses how the structural condition becomes readable as a projected effect, risk transition, damping mode, drift field, or regime state.

## V4 — Decision or Evidence Surface

$V_4$ identifies what type of decision, assessment, evidence trace, or governance interpretation becomes possible once the structure is classified.

## Public Scope

The V1-V4 grammar is public. It does not define:

- customer-specific operator selection;
- vendor telemetry mapping;
- quantitative scoring;
- production thresholds;
- intervention instructions;
- execution-engine behavior.

It defines only the structural reading path used before evidence protocols or future execution layers attach.
